# Generating every product image costs $20 to $27,000

Generating images for the whole catalogue costs between about **$20 and $27,400**, depending almost entirely on the route. A self-hosted FLUX.1 schnell run on a rented RTX 4090 costs roughly **$0.24-$0.62 per 1,000 images** in GPU time, which is about $7-$17 for one image per product (27,162), $19-$50 for all 81,133 existing product images, and $31-$80 for images plus variants (~129,700). The cheapest reputable hosted route is Google's Nano Banana 2.1 at batch rates, **$16.80 per 1,000 images** ($456 / $1,363 / $2,179 for the three scopes). OpenAI's gpt-image-2 at medium quality costs **$53 per 1,000 images** standard or $26.50 batch ($1,440-$6,874 standard and $720-$3,437 batch). At high quality it costs $211 per 1,000, or $27,367 for the full ~129,700 at standard rates. Every figure is a raw generation cost. Real spend is higher by 1/(1 - rejection rate), and reported AI product-image rejection rates run from 10% to 60%, so budget a **1.3x to 2x multiplier**. Several inputs are unverified or conflict across sources (flagged below). Self-hosting is cheapest in GPU dollars, but engineering and QA time, not GPU time, then dominates the cost.

## The three scopes and a scaling rule

The sizing uses three counts: **(a) 27,162** (one image per product), **(b) 81,133** (all existing product images) and **(c) about 129,700** (81,133 plus 48,568 variant images = 129,701). Cost scales linearly, so each total below is the per-1,000 price multiplied by 27.162, 81.133 or 129.701. For growth, the existing ratios imply about 2.99 product images and 1.79 variants per product. Every 65,000 new products (about 10 GB of synthetic data) therefore adds about 65,000 images for scope (a), about 194,000 for (b) and about 310,000 for (c). Multiply the per-1,000 prices by 65, 194 or 310 to cost one such batch. One assumption is that variant images are generated separately. If many variants can reuse a product's image, the real count sits between (b) and (c).

## Hosted APIs span a 40x price range

OpenAI now bills by token and publishes a per-image table only for older models. At 1024x1024, gpt-image-2 costs **$0.006 / $0.053 / $0.211** (low / medium / high), gpt-image-1.5 costs $0.009 / $0.034 / $0.133, and gpt-image-1-mini costs **$0.005 / $0.011 / $0.036** ([OpenAI image guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). The notes flag a caveat: gpt-image-2, 1.5 and 1-mini are all marked deprecated, with no shutdown dates found, and gpt-image-1 was shut down on 2026-10-23 ([gpt-image-1 page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-image-1)). The current replacements, gpt-image-2.5 and 2.6, have **no published per-image table**. They share gpt-image-2's token rates, $30 per million image-output tokens ([pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)), so gpt-image-2 prices are only a proxy. The 2.5 line also adds xhigh and max tiers whose cost is unknown. The Batch API gives a **50% discount** with a 24-hour window, up to 50,000 requests per file, and covers image generation and edits ([Batch guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch)). One conflict is unresolved: the pricing page lists batch rates for the 2.6 models, but their model pages say batch is "Not supported" ([2.6-sunburst page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-image-2.6-sunburst)).

Google's Gemini image models are the strongest hosted value. Nano Banana 2.1 costs $0.0336 at 1K resolution and **$0.0168 in batch**. Nano Banana 2 Lite has the same prices. Nano Banana 2 costs $0.067 at 1K with no batch price listed, and Nano Banana Pro costs $0.134 ([AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). The older Nano Banana (2.5 Flash Image) costs $0.039 but is scheduled to shut down on 2027-03-15. Imagen 4 ($0.02-$0.06) may already be retired, since sources conflict, so do not plan on it. On the other providers, fal.ai bills FLUX.2 at $0.012 per megapixel and FLUX.2 Flash at $0.005 ([fal.ai](https://fal.ai/pricing)). Black Forest Labs bills FLUX.2 klein at $0.014-$0.015 and FLUX.2 pro at $0.03 for the first megapixel ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/pricing)). Stability charges $0.03 for Core, $0.065 for SD3.5 Large and $0.08 for Ultra ([Stability](https://docs.stability.ai/pricing)). Replicate lists FLUX Schnell at $3.00 per 1,000 and FLUX 1.1 Pro at $0.04 ([Replicate](https://replicate.com/pricing)). Batch discounts exist only at Google and OpenAI. BFL, Stability, fal and Replicate publish no tiers and offer negotiated enterprise pricing. Midjourney has no official API and is not viable.

## Self-hosting cuts GPU cost 10-100x but moves the work to you

On rented GPUs, FLUX.1 schnell (4 steps) runs about 1-4 s per image on 4090, A100 and H100 cards and 6-10 s on an L4, while SDXL on a 4090 takes about 2.6 s ([JarvisLabs](https://jarvislabs.ai/ai-faqs/best-gpu-for-flux), [dev.to benchmark](https://dev.to/jovan_chan_9500711396d4e6/flux-vs-sdxl-vs-sd-15-real-cost-per-image-across-gpus-2026-44f8)). The benchmarks disagree. One 4090 schnell figure is 1.8 s, another is 4-5 s, and a third implies about 12 s, so treat every speed as a range. At RunPod prices of $0.34/hr (4090 Community), $0.89 (4090 Secure) and $1.19-$2.09 (A100 PCIe) ([RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-cloud/pricing)), the arithmetic in the notes gives $0.24-$0.62 per 1,000 for schnell or SDXL on a 4090, about $1-1.8 on an A100 and about $1.1-2.0 on an H100. Hyperscaler GPUs cost more, at about $1.40-$1.80 per 1,000 on AWS g5/g6 or GCP L4. FLUX.1 dev is roughly 4-20x costlier, at $0.94-$9 per 1,000. These per-1,000 prices are my midpoint estimates, not measured throughput. A 2x error in seconds per image doubles the cost.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLUX schnell, 4090 Community ($0.34/hr) | $0.24 | $7 | $19 | $31 |
| FLUX schnell, 4090 Secure ($0.89/hr) | $0.62 | $17 | $50 | $80 |
| FLUX schnell, L4/A10G on AWS or GCP | $1.40-$1.80 | $38-$49 | $114-$146 | $182-$233 |
| FLUX dev, H100 Community | $5.60 | $152 | $454 | $726 |

Wall-clock time is the other axis. A single 4090 running schnell takes about **19 hours for (a), 56 hours for (b) and 90 hours for (c)**, and eight GPUs cut those to about 2.4, 7 and 11 hours at the same total cost, since the work is embarrassingly parallel. OpenAI synchronous generation is limited by images per minute. At the 20 IPM Build tier, scopes (a), (b) and (c) take about 23, 68 and 108 hours. At the 250 IPM Grow tier they take about 1.8, 5.4 and 8.6 hours ([gpt-image-2 page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-image-2)). The Batch API completes within 24 hours per file, and scope (c) needs three files. The notes found no real-world per-image latency for OpenAI. BFL allows 24 concurrent requests, so at an assumed 5-10 s per image (my assumption) scope (c) would take roughly 3-7 hours ([BFL guidelines](https://docs.bfl.ai/api_integration/integration_guidelines)).

Licensing decides which open models are usable. FLUX.1 schnell is Apache-2.0 and cleared for commercial use ([HF](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.1-schnell)). FLUX.1 dev is non-commercial and bars use "for any commercial or production purposes" without a BFL license, although outputs themselves may be used commercially ([BFL license](https://bfl.ai/legal/non-commercial-license-terms)). SD 3.5 is free for organizations under $1M annual revenue with registration and a "Powered by Stability AI" notice ([Stability license](https://stability.ai/community-license-agreement)). The SDXL license text was not verified. On the hosted side, Google outputs carry an invisible SynthID watermark, and the notes did not read the commercial-use terms for Gemini, Stability, fal or Replicate. BFL's commercial tiers cover self-hosted open weights only (10K-100K images/month for the lower tiers) ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/pricing)). This is not legal advice.

## Rejects multiply every price by 1.1x to 2.5x

The reject multiplier is 1/(1 - r): 1.11x at 10% rejected, 1.33x at 25% and 2x at 50%. The published evidence is weak and mostly vendor or seller blogs. One source reports about 23% of AI product images with detectable artifacts, and one small seller sample found 40-60% of AI lifestyle images failing at least one QA check ([Bria](https://bria.ai/blog/wh-ai-product-images-get-rejected), [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/what-i-learned-about-ai-product-images-from-rejecting-half-the-output-zXjaoGXp3OK6wpPNfcOd)). Transparent, reflective, jewelry and textile products and anything with small text fail more often. Applied to Nano Banana 2.1 batch for scope (c), $2,179 becomes about **$2,900 at 25% rejection and $4,360 at 50%**. Self-hosted schnell on a 4090 Secure rises from $80 to about $107-$160. The multiplier applies to every route. It hits hosted APIs harder in dollars, because retries are billed per image.

OpenAI's moderation filter adds a separate retry path. A blocked request returns `moderation_blocked`, and a `moderation: low` setting is available ([OpenAI guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation)). Branded products, people and apparel could trigger blocks, but this is an inference that the docs did not confirm. Self-hosted fidelity to real SKUs probably needs img2img, ControlNet or LoRAs, which the notes estimate (unsourced) could raise per-image time 2-4x. The largest self-hosting cost is engineering: a pipeline, queue with checkpointing, storage and automated QA (OCR, CLIP, VLM judge). The notes found no sourced hours or dollars for this, and a few engineer-days to a couple of weeks is my estimate only.

## Unverified and conflicting figures

Treat these as open items. OpenAI gpt-image-2.5 and 2.6 per-image prices are unpublished (the gpt-image-2 table is a proxy and its own 1024x1536 price is lower than 1024x1024, which was not verified). The 2.6 batch support conflict is unresolved. The deprecation dates for gpt-image-2, 1.5 and 1-mini are unknown. The Imagen 4 and Nova Canvas retirement claims (the latter from one aggregator) are unverified. Nova Canvas prices ($0.04/$0.06) come from secondary sources only, and the Vertex AI page, BFL "FLUX 3" prices and Replicate's FLUX.2 pro price were not retrieved. All self-hosted speeds are from blogs with unclear methodology, the gigagpu figures came from search snippets only, and the Valebyte benchmark was excluded as not credible. Rejection rates have no independent, methodology-backed source. Prices were read on 2026-11-15, and Lambda's page showed rates effective 2026-11-16.

## Conclusion

The cost question collapses to a quality question. Raw generation for the full ~129,700 images is under $100 self-hosted, about $650-$2,200 on the cheapest reputable APIs and $7,000-$27,000 on premium OpenAI tiers. Even a 2x reject multiplier does not change that ordering, so choose on fidelity and operational burden, not on API price. The 100x gap between self-hosting and premium APIs is mostly a gap in who carries the engineering and QA work.

The recommendation is a staged approach. First, run a 200-SKU pilot comparing Google Nano Banana 2.1 (batch), fal FLUX.2 and self-hosted FLUX.1 schnell on a 4090 (pilot cost is a few dollars), and measure real rejection rates by product type. For scope (a), one image per product, Nano Banana 2.1 batch costs about **$456 (about $600 at 25% rejects)** with no infrastructure. If scale grows toward hundreds of thousands of images per 10 GB data batch, move to self-hosted schnell (Apache-2.0, clearest licensing) with checkpointed jobs, keeping a hosted API as the fallback for failed or high-fidelity items. Avoid OpenAI high quality and deprecated models for bulk work, avoid FLUX.1 dev without a BFL license, and avoid Midjourney. Before committing, resolve the gpt-image-2.5 pricing if OpenAI is under consideration.
